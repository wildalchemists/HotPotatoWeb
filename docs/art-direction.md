# Art direction

Every decision below cites a source: a screenshot, the key art, the logo, a mechanic, or the team. "Looks modern" is not a source. Palette values come from `scripts/palette.mjs` (output in `docs/palette.json`); the images it read are the seven PNGs recovered from the old page (`assets/img/legacy/`, regenerated by `scripts/extract-legacy.mjs`).

### 1.3 Type evidence

The logo uses two letterings in one lock-up: **HOT** is an angular, spiky spray outline with drips and arrows (the "hot" half), **POTATO** is a fat throw-up bubble style with faces drawn into the letters (the "potato" half). Both sit on corrugated container walls at an angle. Any type choice has to answer to one of those two, or to the containers themselves (stencilled ID codes, hazard markings). The old site's Lilita One + Special Elite pair answered to neither.

### 1.4 The mechanic

One player is "it" and *is* the potato (the tagged runner turns into the potato character, see map-1-thumb: one potato, many runners). The potato passes by tagging. Whoever holds it when the timer hits zero loses the round. The verbs are: tag, pass, chase, run, count down. Every motion on the page is allowed to mean one of those and nothing else.

## 2. Three directions

### A — Container Yard

- **Idea.** The arena is the page: the site is built out of the containers the game is built out of.
- **Palette.** Sky `#3BABD2` hero, concrete `#DADDE3` sections, green `#268741` frames, potato yellow `#E8C73B` for every CTA, ink `#0B1230` lines. Hazard stripe (`--yellow` + `--ink`) used once, under the hero.
- **Type.** *Big Shoulders Stencil Display* 900 for display — a stencil answers to the ID codes stencilled on every real container and to the blocky painted letters on the game's containers. *Archivo* for text (industrial grotesque, wide counters, reads at 1rem). *IBM Plex Mono* for cargo codes, statuses and table headers.
- **Layout logic.** Cargo-label grid: a 3 px ink rule everywhere, labels pinned to the top edge of boxes (`HPTU 365019 0 · 45G1`: the Steam app id as a container number), section markers as vertical manifest codes, modes as a shipping manifest table.
- **Texture.** Corrugation as an 8 px repeating gradient at 14 % black on green and yellow surfaces only; never on text areas. Hard offset shadows (no blur), as paint on steel.
- **Signature interaction.** The hero image sits inside a real container; its doors are half open and swing fully open on hover/focus (`rotateY` on two door elements). Nothing else moves.
- **Risk.** Can drift into "industrial SaaS" if the stencil is used for body text or the hazard stripe multiplies. The container metaphor is literal; it tells the setting more than the joke of the game.

### B — Tagged

- **Idea.** The graffiti logo is the system: every section is a wall that got tagged, every screenshot is a poster slapped on with tape, the team's own hand-drawn arrows point at things.
- **Palette.** Walls in the container colours: red `#A51F1B` (hero), potato yellow `#E8C73B`, runner purple `#6336C2`; paper `#F4F2EC` stickers; ink `#0B1230` outlines and offset shadows.
- **Type.** *Rubik Spray Paint* for display — a spray-painted face that answers to the HOT half of the logo. *Rubik* for text (same family, so the sticker copy and the sprayed title share a skeleton). *Permanent Marker* only for hand-written notes and roles, as a marker on a wall.
- **Layout logic.** Nothing is aligned on purpose: posters at −4° to +3°, stickers at ±2°, notes written over edges. The grid still exists underneath (two columns, 1360 px max) so it holds on mobile.
- **Texture.** Corrugation on every wall (12/40 px stripes at 14–18 % black), paper stickers with 3 px ink border and 5 px offset shadow, translucent tape corners, hand-drawn SVG arrows and a hand-drawn circle around the trailer link (paths with deliberate wobble, not geometry).
- **Signature interaction.** The circle around "watch the trailer" draws itself on load (`stroke-dashoffset`); posters straighten on hover as if you pressed them flat. Both off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Risk.** It is the most demanding to keep consistent as content grows (every new item needs its angle and tape). Rubik Spray Paint's texture needs ≥ 2.5 rem to stay legible, so small headings fall back to Rubik 900. It leans on the key art crops, which are brightened versions of a dark file; a clean logo from the team removes that dependency.

### C — Match Broadcast

- **Idea.** The page is a party-game broadcast of one round: a live feed with a timer, a "YOU'RE IT" callout, a scoreboard for the modes, a ticker for the awards.
- **Palette.** Ink `#0B1230` chrome, the game's own sky and yard as the feed, potato yellow `#E8C73B` for timer and CTAs, runner purple `#6336C2` for the callout, sneaker red `#DD3714` for LIVE.
- **Type.** *Anton* for display — condensed sports-broadcast lettering, the HUD voice. *Chivo* for text, *Chivo Mono* for numbers (timer, round, codes) so digits are tabular.
- **Layout logic.** Broadcast chrome: HUD corners on the feed, a lower third for name + pitch + CTA, a scoreboard list for modes with rank numbers, a ticker strip. Dark page, bright feed.
- **Texture.** None; flat chrome at 85 % ink over the image, 1 px lines `#2A3566`. The only glow is on the fuse.
- **Signature interaction.** The fuse: a yellow line across the top of the page burns from left to right as you scroll (`animation-timeline: scroll()`, static fallback). "YOU'RE IT" pops once on load. The ticker scrolls. All off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Risk.** The HUD is invented: no in-game HUD exists in the assets, so the timer and round counter are our fiction, and the game may ship with a different one. Dark pages read "competitive shooter" more than "party game"; the game's own frames are bright. Three moving things on one hero is the maximum this direction can afford.

### A fourth was considered and not drawn

"Potato POV": the page as the potato's own countdown, everything fuse and heat. The assets do not support it: the potato on fire appears only in the key art (dark, laurelled) and in no screenshot, so there is no imagery to carry it beyond the hero.

## 3. Mockups

`mockups/a.html`, `mockups/b.html`, `mockups/c.html`: static, no build step, open from disk. Real copy from `content/site.json` (EN), real assets from `assets/img/` (WebP, 1200/2400 srcset). Each shows the hero (name, pitch, Wishlist on Steam above the fold, trailer entry point, awards) plus one dense section: A and C show **Modes**, B shows **Roles**. Missing facts stay as visible dashed `TODO` chips rather than invented lines.

Derived assets in `mockups/assets/` are crops of `hero-art` with a +60–75 % brightness lift and the laurels cropped out. They exist only to show the logo on a page; the real build needs the clean logo (`OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` #8).

Screenshots at 1440 and 390 are in `docs/mockups/`. Checked with Playwright: no horizontal scroll at 320, 390, 768, 1440 and 2560; all fonts loaded from Google Fonts; nav links collapse below 900 px (the real build gets a menu button there).

## 4. Ban list

Obeyed from here on, in templates, CSS and copy. `scripts/check.mjs` (Prompt 5) warns on the copy words.

- emoji as icons or bullets; generic icon sets used decoratively
- eyebrow label + huge title + short coloured bar + grey subtitle repeated on every section
- gradient hairline dividers between sections; glassmorphism nav; soft drop shadows on every card
- fade-up-on-scroll on everything; hover-lift on every card; parallax for its own sake
- three equal feature cards in a row; centred everything; 8 px radius on everything
- Inter, Nunito, Poppins, Montserrat; the old Lilita One + Special Elite pair
- gradient placeholder boxes; fake stats; testimonials without a real source
- copy words: unleash, epic, ultimate, chaos-filled journey, "experience the", "get ready to"

Instead: asymmetry, real screenshots large and cropped with intent, motion only where it means something (the fuse, a tag, a pass), one signature moment per page.

Allowed and not to be confused with the list: a scrim gradient under text on a photo (C's lower third), an offset hard shadow (A, B), one hazard stripe (A).

## 5. Recommendation

**B (Tagged)**, with C's fuse as the scroll-progress line. B is the only direction whose every element traces to the logo itself, and it is the one that looks hand-made at 390 px, where A becomes a table and C becomes a dark app. Its cost is discipline: angles and tape are a system, not decoration, so the build gets a fixed set of three rotations and two tape positions.

## 6. Tokens for Prompt 3 (whichever direction is picked)

```css
:root{
  --sky:#3BABD2; --sky-deep:#1987AC; --blue:#154396;
  --green:#268741; --green-deep:#226C28;
  --yellow:#E8C73B; --cyellow:#C7851A; --orange:#CD9226;
  --red:#DD3714; --red-deep:#961B16;
  --purple:#6336C2; --purple-lit:#A744CD;
  --concrete:#B8BDCA; --concrete-lt:#DADDE3; --ink:#0B1230; --paper:#F3F2EE;
  /* type scale, fluid */
  --step--1:clamp(.84rem,.8rem+.2vw,.95rem);
  --step-0:clamp(1rem,.95rem+.3vw,1.125rem);
  --step-1:clamp(1.15rem,1.05rem+.6vw,1.5rem);
  --step-2:clamp(1.6rem,1.3rem+1.6vw,2.4rem);
  --step-3:clamp(2.6rem,1.8rem+4vw,4.5rem);
  --step-4:clamp(4.4rem,2rem+11vw,11rem);
}
```

Contrast checked (WCAG formula) for body text: `--ink` on `--paper` 16.4:1, `--ink` on `--sky` 6.9:1, `--ink` on `--yellow` 11.1:1, `--ink` on `--concrete-lt` 13.5:1, `--paper` on `--ink` 16.4:1, `--paper` on `--purple` 6.6:1, `--paper` on `--wall-red` 6.7:1. All above 4.5:1.
